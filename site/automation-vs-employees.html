Automation vs. Employees

Before you post another job listing, read this.

Hiring isn't the only way to get the busywork off your plate. Here's an honest look at what each option actually costs, how fast it starts paying off, and where a person still beats a workflow.

The real cost of hiring

A new hire costs more than the number on the offer letter.

Salary is just the headline number. By the time you add payroll tax, benefits, equipment, training time, and management attention, the real cost runs well past what's on paper, and that's before anyone's actually productive.

Where a person still wins

Automation isn't the answer to everything, and we'll tell you when it isn't.

Anything that needs real judgment, a relationship, or creative thinking still needs a person behind it. Automation is good at the predictable part: the same steps, the same inputs, the same outcome, every time. The moment a task needs someone to read between the lines, that's a job for your team, not a workflow.

Good reasons to hire

  1. The work needs judgment calls that change case by case
  2. Clients or customers expect a real relationship, not a form
  3. The task is creative, strategic, or genuinely one-off
  4. You need someone physically present

Good reasons to automate first

  1. The steps are the same every time, regardless of who's doing it
  2. It's high-volume but low-complexity
  3. It only happens because someone has to remember to do it
  4. Mistakes happen because a person got busy or distracted
03

A realistic path

Most businesses end up doing both. Just not in that order.

The businesses that get the most out of this usually automate the repetitive stuff first, then hire for what's left: the relationship work, the judgment calls, the parts that actually grow the business. Hiring to cover busywork tends to mean you've hired someone whose best hours go to tasks a workflow could've handled.

We'll tell you straight if a task you're asking about is better suited to a person than a workflow. That's not us talking ourselves out of business, it's the only way this is worth doing for either of us.
